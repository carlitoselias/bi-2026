04
The hero’s journey

What changes during the journey?

Popularized by Joseph Campbell, this framework describes a recurring narrative pattern in myths and legends: a hero leaves the ordinary world, faces challenges and returns transformed, bringing an “elixir” to the community.

In data science, it offers a structure for presenting the analytical journey, from the initial problem to a solution and its impact.

05
The hero’s journey

You are not the hero

In a classic story, the hero is the one who goes through the journey. In data storytelling, the audience is the hero.

Your role is not to impress people with sophisticated techniques. Your role is to guide them from confusion to understanding, and from understanding to action.

Stage 1 · The ordinary world

A city planning team wants to understand why people are having fewer children. Start with the change the team can verify.

Across OECD countries, the average fertility rate fell from 3.3 children per woman in 1960 to 1.5 in 2022.

The rate describes how many children a woman would have if that year’s age-specific birth rates held throughout her life.

Economic development has often accompanied smaller families. Yet prosperous countries can have very different fertility rates.

Why was the 2022 rate nearly four times as high in Israel as in Korea?

The contrast gives the planning team a reason to look beyond national prosperity.

The hero’s journey

Stage 3 · Refusal of the call

Before accepting an explanation, the planning team asks three questions.

Noise

Could this be a one-year dip?

Check the annual series for a sustained change.

Evidence

Whose experience does the average hide?

Compare country rates and ask households about their choices.

Method

Are we comparing like with like?

Use the same year and the same definition in both countries.

The hero’s journey

Stage 4 · Allies and tests

Is the OECD average a good guide to what happens in each high-income country?

The long-term change

The OECD average dropped from 3.3 children per woman in 1960 to 1.5 in 2022.

The test across countries

In 2022 Israel recorded 2.9 and Korea about 0.78. A high-income economy can have either a relatively high or a very low rate.

These figures measure births. To learn whether people had the children they wanted, the team needs to ask them.

Stage 5 · The supreme ordeal

A prosperous economy can still leave people unable to afford the family they want.

In a UNFPA/YouGov survey across 14 countries, 39% of respondents said financial limits had affected or would affect their ability to have their desired number of children.

Housing and childcare costs are two expenses a city can investigate. The survey does not assign either one a share of the fertility decline.

The hero’s journey

Stage 6 · Return with the elixir

Ask local families which cost gets in the way. Use their answers to choose one barrier the city can reduce.

If childcare fees come up most often, pilot a fee reduction. If rent is the main obstacle, test housing support instead.

Follow up with participants. Did the expense fall? Do they feel better able to make the family choices they want?

17
Three acts

Where does the tension peak?

Set an expectation, show what breaks it, then give the audience a way forward.

I · Setup

What does the audience expect?

II · Conflict

Which fact challenges that expectation?

III · Resolution

What decision follows?

This structure comes from drama. Tension peaks when the evidence breaks the audience’s expectation.

18
Three acts

The visual narrative arc

  1. Beginning

    Present the setting, establish normal conditions and define expectations.

  2. Tension

    Something unexpected appears: an anomaly or a change that breaks the pattern.

  3. Climax

    The point of greatest interest, where the scale of the change becomes clear.

  4. Resolution

    Explain the phenomenon and propose an interpretation supported by evidence.

  5. Closing

    State implications, recommended actions and strategic next steps.

Data can support a narrative rhythm similar to a literary story. The structure helps maintain attention and makes the message easier to remember.

Three acts

Act II · Show what breaks the expectation

An audience expects longer delivery times to be a network-wide problem. The teaching case shows that three logistics centers account for 75% of delays.

Make the contrast visible

Put the concentration of delays beside the original expectation. Now the reader has a reason to ask what happens at those centers.

Keep the claim in bounds

The share tells us where delays occurred. It does not identify their cause or establish statistical significance.

21
Three acts

Act III · Resolution

The story should end clearly:

Meaning

“This means that…”

Recommendation

“Therefore, we recommend…”

Decision

“The best decision under these conditions is…”

A resolution connects the analysis to its practical meaning and the action it can support.

Three acts

E-commerce improves delivery

Act I · Introduction

In 2023, Trustpilot ratings fall from 4.3 to 3.5 because of delivery delays. Operations is overwhelmed, and customers criticize delivery times.

Act II · Development

Analysis finds that 75% of delays come from 3 logistics centers. The delivery promise is adjusted to location and actual inventory availability.

Act III · Resolution

Ratings rise to 4.1 in 45 days. On-time deliveries increase from 68% to 91%. The team presents the evidence to product management to scale the approach.

Small operational changes, guided by analysis and presented as a clear narrative, can improve customer satisfaction and operational performance.

And, But, Therefore

What changes at “but”?

This compact structure helps communicate complex ideas in science, policy and business. It distills dense information into a clear, persuasive argument and is used in scientific articles, policy reports and business presentations.

01

AND

Established context.

02

BUT

A revealed tension.

03

THEREFORE

A recommended action.

A useful framework for executive reports.

25
And, But, Therefore

ABT applied to data

“Attendance at the cultural center was growing steadily each week throughout the quarter, but it fell sharply by 35% during the special anniversary event; therefore, public transport and parking services should be adjusted for large events.”

The ABT structure turns raw observations into a narrative with a finding (the decline), a break (the special event) and a proposed implication (adjust transport).

Does the 35% decline establish a transport problem?

No. The example illustrates a narrative structure. The decline alone does not establish its cause. Transport and parking remain hypotheses to investigate before making that recommendation.

And, But, Therefore

Digital banking

AND

The digital banking app has more than 200K downloads per month and provides a fast, branch-free experience.

BUT

Funnel data show that only 18% of users completed account opening.

THEREFORE

The team redesigns the process with dynamic forms and push notifications. Conversion rises to 41% in 30 days.

ABT presents a business problem, the response and the reported result in a compact sequence.

Setup → Insight → Implication

Improving last-mile logistics

Setup · Stable average cost

The cost per delivery has remained at $2.90 per package over the last two quarters.

Insight · Regional differences

The regional breakdown shows $4.20 in suburban areas and $2.10 in dense areas. The example attributes the difference to a routing change that prioritizes speed over efficiency.

Implication · Optimization

Revising routing parameters and segmenting by area could reduce total cost by 11% without affecting delivery times.

A finer level of analysis reveals a problem hidden by the average and an opportunity to improve operations.

31
What, why, and what it means

What can the venue director conclude?

The venue director asks whether to add evening places before the next event.

What happened?

Across all sessions, ticket requests rose 18% in the two weeks after the event. In the evening, more people requested places than the venue could seat.

Why might it have happened?

The event may have attracted visitors. Evenings may also be busy in ordinary weeks. Compare the same evenings in weeks without an event to check that explanation.

What can we decide?

Review evening capacity first. Count unfilled requests for each session to size a trial. The 18% describes all requests, so it cannot tell the director how many evening seats to add.

The inverted pyramid

Give the reader the conclusion first. Add detail in order of importance.

33
The inverted pyramid

How much does the reader need?

  1. Main message first

    Lead with the conclusion.

  2. Direct evidence next

    Show the support for that conclusion.

  3. Technical detail last

    Provide the context needed to evaluate it.

Useful in dashboards and reports: it saves reading time and reduces the load on the audience.

Read the evidence and context

Conclusion · Higher revenue

Dynamic pricing increased revenue per room by 19% in the first 2 months.

Evidence · ADR and occupancy

Compared with the previous equivalent period, average daily rate (ADR) rose from $86 to $102. Occupancy remained at 71%. Data came from the property management system (PMS) and booking engine.

Context · From manual pricing to AI

Prices were previously updated manually every 48 hours, without incorporating demand, weather or events. The new solution uses a machine-learning prediction model.
